I · The invitation

Beautiful surroundings are never the destination.

They are invitations to slow down, to engage, to discover, and to belong.

Born on the shores of Lake Geneva and carried from Zürich to Dubai, the House now sets its table in California — at a hotel that has been holding the city's conversations since 1912. The Evening is the smaller of our gatherings: the one that stays in the room.

II · The shape of the night

Golden hour, then the table, then the fire.

First

Arrival with the light

Up the palm drive off Sunset as the light turns. A swim for those who want it, an aperitivo under the trees for those who don't. Nothing begins on a schedule.

Second

A chef-led dinner

One long table, and no second one. The evening is carried by a single woven conversation — a question posed with each course, answered around the whole table rather than in pairs.

Third

Digestif by fire

The table dissolves into the garden. A last glass, unhurried, no closing remarks. The evening ends itself.

The setting

The Beverly Hills Hotel.

The Pink Palace on Sunset Boulevard — open since 1912, and for a century the room where Los Angeles has done its talking. Palms, bungalows, gardens that hold a conversation without amplifying it. We take one table in it.

9641 Sunset Boulevard, Beverly Hills, California

The table · one conversation

Before dinner · the water

No stage. No name tags. Nothing performed.

What is newest in longevity comes to the table the way everything else does — carried by the people building it, placed among the guests rather than presented at them, and open to the question that follows.

The rarest luxury is not exclusivity. It is finding yourself among people who leave you more inspired than when you arrived.

III · Who sits at the table

Every seat is invited by name and earned by work.

Builders and creators — of companies, of culture, of science, of what the body can do — and the investors who back them. People who make things, gather people, and follow their curiosity further than most.
